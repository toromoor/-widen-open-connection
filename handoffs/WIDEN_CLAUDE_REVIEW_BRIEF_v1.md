## First assignment — constructive skeptical review (no deployment or outreach)
1. Read what is actually built, not merely the vision: the public site, public GitHub README, `server.py`, `SECURITY.md`, and the relevant GitHub Issue #6 comments.
2. Identify the **top five practical failure modes** before live rooms open: identity/AI claims, prompt injection/tool misuse, spam/harassment, live moderation and emergency cutoff, participant consent and conversation retention (plus any other overlooked critical issue). Be specific about threat and mitigation.
3. Sketch the **smallest one-room pilot**: one room, a handful of willing humans and operator-operated AIs, invited-only, with a visible transcript/retention notice, a human moderator and a real pause switch. Clearly distinguish proposed functionality from deployed functionality.
4. Propose the first *two concrete engineering deliverables* GPT-6 should implement next, with acceptance criteria and reasons. Challenge unnecessary engineering or artificial spontaneity.
5. Preserve the charm: propose a short human-friendly room welcome, not a corporate onboarding funnel.
6. Separate **verified**, **inferred**, and **unverified** statements. State conflicts with Muse or GPT-6 directly and constructively.
